Fundamental Theorem

The Master Pricing Identity

$$\text{Price}_{\text{Structured Note}} = \text{ZCB}(0, T) \times \left(1 + \text{Funding Spread}\right) + \sum_{i=1}^M w_i \cdot \text{Option}_i(S, K_i, T) + \text{Issuer Margin}$$
Component Anatomy
  • $\text{ZCB}(0, T)$: Zero-Coupon Bond discounting par redemption ($100\%$) over maturity $T$ at the issuer's funding rate.
  • Funding Spread: The desk's funding margin (e.g., SOFR $+ 75\text{ bps}$). Higher issuer credit spread leaves more budget to buy derivative options.
  • $\sum w_i \cdot \text{Option}_i$: The derivative engine (calls, put spreads, digitals, barrier options) generating the desired payoff profile.
  • Issuer Margin: Structuring desk + distributor commission (typically $1.0\% - 3.5\%$ upfront).
Desk Hedging Intuition
  • Self-Funding Principle: The bond leg is kept on the bank's treasury book to provide cheap unsecured term funding.
  • Option Leg Carve-Out: The derivative component is passed directly to the exotics trading desk to be dynamically hedged or repackaged.
  • The Rate-Vol Tradeoff: When interest rates rise, $\text{ZCB}$ cost drops, freeing up substantial budget to enhance coupon yields or participation rates.
Interview Pitch Question
  • "Why did structured products flourish in high-rate environments?"
  • Answer: When risk-free rates are $5\%$, a 3-year ZCB costs $\approx \$86.38$. The bank has $\$13.62$ of option budget to buy exotic calls or offer generous guaranteed coupons, compared to only $\$2.90$ in a $1\%$ rate world.
Yield Enhancement

Barrier Reverse Convertible (BRC) Replication

$$\text{Payoff}(S_T) = \text{Coupon} + \begin{cases} 100\% & \text{if } \min_{t \in [0, T]} S_t > B \text{ or } S_T \ge S_0 \\ \frac{S_T}{S_0} \times 100\% & \text{if } \min_{t \in [0, T]} S_t \le B \text{ and } S_T < S_0 \end{cases}$$
Financial Engineering Formula
  • Replication Formula: $\text{BRC} = \text{Fixed Bond} - \text{Down-and-In Put Option}\left(K = S_0, B = 65\%\right)$.
  • Coupon Source: The attractive coupon (e.g., $9\% - 12\%$) is financed by the premium collected from selling the down-and-in put.
  • American vs European Barrier: American barriers monitor continuous intraday lows; European barriers monitor only the final observation $S_T$.
Desk Risk Profile
  • Short Volatility: The investor is short vega and short skew. The desk is long volatility and long skew.
  • Asymmetric Risk: The investor caps upside at the coupon but participates $1:1$ in severe market crashes if the barrier breaches.
  • Autocall Conversion: Adding early call levels converts the static BRC into an express or Phoenix autocallable.
Interview Takeaway
  • "Who buys a Barrier Reverse Convertible and when?"
  • Answer: Moderately bullish to sideways investors who anticipate range-bound equities and want above-market fixed income yields while accepting downside tail equity risk below the barrier.
Wealth Management Core

Phoenix Autocallable & Memory Mechanics

$$\text{Observation } t_k: \quad \text{If } S_{t_k} \ge K_{\text{call}} \implies \text{Early Redemption: } 100\% + \text{Coupon}(t_k) + \sum_{\text{unpaid } j} \text{Coupon}(t_j)$$
Structural Rules
  • Autocall Trigger ($K_{\text{call}}$): Typically $100\%$ initially, often stepping down by $2\% - 5\%$ per semester (Step-Down Phoenix).
  • Coupon Barrier ($B_{\text{coupon}}$): Typically $70\% - 80\%$. If underlying is above this barrier, the periodic coupon is paid.
  • Memory Feature: Any missed coupons from prior periods when $S < B_{\text{coupon}}$ are caught up and paid in full when spot recovers.
  • Protection Barrier ($B_{\text{prot}}$): Final safety buffer (e.g. $60\%$) at final maturity $T$.
Trading Desk Hedging Dynamics
  • Duration Uncertainty: Effective duration depends on whether the product calls. As spot rises, duration collapses towards the next observation date.
  • Gamma Sign Flip: Positive gamma far from barriers turns into violent negative gamma as spot hovers near the barrier close to maturity.
  • Autocall Recycling: High autocall redemptions force retail wealth desks to immediately reinvest into new issuance, driving continuous primary supply.
Structuring Interview Drill
  • "What is the impact of a step-down autocall barrier on the coupon?"
  • Answer: Step-downs (e.g. $100\% \to 95\% \to 90\%$) increase the probability of early redemption, which reduces the expected duration and slightly lowers the offered coupon, but substantially enhances investor safety.
Multi-Asset Engineering

Worst-Of Basket Mechanics & Correlation Sensitivity

$$\text{Basket Performance} = \min_{i \in \{1,\dots,N\}} \left( \frac{S_i(t)}{S_i(0)} \right), \quad \frac{\partial \text{Worst-Of Put Value}}{\partial \rho_{i,j}} < 0$$
Mathematical Framework
  • Worst-Of Feature: Payoff depends strictly on the laggard of $3 - 4$ stocks (e.g., LVMH, TotalEnergies, ASML).
  • The "Free Lunch" Illusion: Adding more underlyings drastically increases the offered coupon (e.g. $7\% \to 13\%$), leading retail investors to assume they get free yield.
  • Correlation Dependence: When $\rho_{ij} = 1$, basket risk equals single-stock risk. When $\rho_{ij} \to -1$, the probability of at least one stock breaching the barrier surges towards $100\%$.
Exotic Desk Correlation Book
  • Desk is Long Correlation: The client is short worst-of put $\implies$ client is short correlation. The desk is long correlation ($\partial V / \partial \rho > 0$).
  • Correlation Smile: Exotic desks must mark cross-asset implied correlations with a skew, reflecting that correlations spike towards $+1$ during systemic crashes.
  • Dispersion Trading: Desks hedge worst-of correlation risk by trading dispersion (long single-stock options vs short index options).
Desk Trap to Avoid
  • "Why do 4-stock worst-of products fail during idiosyncratic crises?"
  • Answer: It only takes one constituent to suffer a corporate scandal or profit warning. If 3 stocks gain $+30\%$ and 1 stock drops $-45\%$, the investor suffers a $-45\%$ capital loss despite a raging bull market.
Rates Derivatives

Constant Maturity Swap (CMS) Steepener Notes

$$\text{Coupon}(t) = \max\left( \text{Floor}, \; L \times \left( \text{CMS}_{10\text{Y}}(t) - \text{CMS}_{2\text{Y}}(t) \right) + \text{Spread} \right)$$
Parameter Definitions
  • $\text{CMS}_{10\text{Y}} - \text{CMS}_{2\text{Y}}$: Benchmark yield curve slope. Reflects term premium and long-term economic growth expectations.
  • $L$ (Leverage Factor): Typically $3\times$ to $5\times$ leverage to magnify small basis point curve shifts into meaningful coupon returns.
  • Convexity Adjustment: CMS rates pay an annuity-based swap rate on a single period, requiring an upward convexity adjustment: $\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[\text{CMS}] > \text{Forward Swap Rate}$.
Macro Rates Strategy
  • The Inversion Danger: During central bank hiking cycles, the yield curve inverts ($\text{CMS}_{10\text{Y}} - \text{CMS}_{2\text{Y}} < 0$), forcing the coupon to hit the floor (often $0\%$).
  • Cap & Floor Hedging: Desks hedge CMS notes using swaption vol surfaces and CMS spread options via the SABR model.
  • Callable CMS: Issuers embed a bermudan call option, giving them the right to redeem the bond when the curve steepens and coupons become expensive.
Rates Structuring Q&A
  • "Why does an investor buy a CMS steepener over a plain floating note?"
  • Answer: An institutional asset manager anticipating central bank rate cuts buys CMS steepeners to capture the rapid dis-inversion and bull steepening of the short end with $4\times$ coupon leverage.
Exotic Desk Risk

Discontinuous Barrier Greeks & Pin Risk

$$\lim_{t \to T^-} \Delta(S_t) = \pm \infty \quad \text{for } S_t \to B^+, \quad \Gamma(S_t) \text{ switches sign instantaneously}$$
Mathematical Singularity
  • Discontinuous Payoff: A barrier option pays a digital binary chunk ($100\%$ par) if intact, but collapses to spot value ($B$) if breached.
  • Delta Spike: As time to expiry $T - t \to 0$, the delta curve approaches a Dirac delta function centered at the barrier level $B$.
  • Vanna & Volga: $\text{Vanna} = \partial \Delta / \partial \sigma$. A spike in implied volatility dramatically shifts the delta and the required physical equity hedge.
The Desk Hedging Nightmare
  • The Short Gamma Whip: Near the barrier, the desk must buy aggressively on rallies and sell into declines. Slippage and transaction costs explode.
  • Smooth Barrier Approximation: Traders rarely hedge a knife-edge barrier directly; they smooth the barrier using a narrow call/put spread or shifted synthetic barrier.
  • Market Impact & Pinning: Massive barrier open interest on bellwether indices (Euro Stoxx 50, S&P 500) can magnetically pin index prices near barrier strikes into the fixing.
Structuring Interview Challenge
  • "What happens to your delta hedge if the spot is 0.5% above a knock-in barrier 1 hour before maturity?"
  • Answer: Delta is massive and highly unstable. If the barrier is breached, delta instantly drops to a vanilla put hedge. To survive pin risk, traders over-hedge or smooth the payoff with liquid vanilla options.

Decomposition Drill
"Decompose a 100% Capital-Protected Shark Note."
Buy a Zero-Coupon Bond yielding $100\%$ at maturity $T$, plus an Up-and-Out Call Option. If the stock rallies without hitting the shark barrier, the client captures upside; if it knocks out, a rebate coupon is paid.
Suitability & Wealth
"Who buys an Autocallable and why?"
Private wealth clients seeking higher yield than sovereign debt in flat/moderately bullish markets. They accept equity drawdowns below barriers in exchange for double-digit coupon income.
Desk Greek Risk
"Why are exotics desks short correlation?"
Retail clients sell worst-of puts to maximize coupons. Therefore, the structuring desk is long worst-of options and structurally short cross-asset correlation. Desks must dynamically hedge via basket dispersion trades.
