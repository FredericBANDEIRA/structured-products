Structuring 101 & Architecture

Deconstruct the DNA of structured notes: the master pricing identity, wrapper legalities, desk funding margins, and secondary market liquidity.

What is a Structured Product?

Institutional Definition

A Structured Product is a synthetic investment instrument engineered by combining an unsecured debt security (typically a Zero-Coupon Bond) with one or more financial derivative contracts (options, swaps, forwards).

Rather than offering a standard fixed or floating coupon, its terminal payoff and periodic cash flows are contractually linked to the performance of one or more underlying assets (equities, interest rate curves, FX crosses, commodities, or credit indices).

🏛️ The Issuer Perspective (The Bank)
  • Cheap Term Funding: The bank issues senior debt directly to wealth clients, accessing funding at rates cheaper than institutional wholesale bond markets ($\text{SOFR} - 25\text{ bps}$ effective).
  • Upfront Fee Extraction: The bank monetizes upfront structuring commissions ($1.0\% - 3.5\%$) plus secondary market trading bid/ask spreads.
  • Flow Recycling: The trading desk absorbs client-sold optionality (e.g., downside put options from Reverse Convertibles) to offset its own institutional books.
💼 The Investor Perspective (The Client)
  • Asymmetric Risk/Reward: Create bespoke payoffs unachievable with physical cash instruments (e.g., $100\%$ capital protection + $80\%$ equity rally participation).
  • Yield Enhancement in Flat Markets: Monetize range-bound or sideways equities to extract $8\% - 14\%$ annual coupons when bonds pay $3\%$.
  • Regulatory & Tax Packaging: Access derivatives and foreign asset classes inside a clean securities account via an ISIN, without requiring complex OTC ISDA documentation.

The European & Swiss Structuring Taxonomy (EUSIPA / SSPA)

The global structuring industry adheres strictly to the four standardized product categories defined by the European Structured Investment Products Association (EUSIPA) and Swiss Structured Products Association (SSPA):

Category Primary Objective Typical Derivative Package Benchmark Products Target Market Profile
Class I: Capital Protection Preserve 100% nominal at maturity + upside participation $\text{ZCB} + \text{Call Option}$ (or Call Spread) Protected Tracker, Shark Note, Capital Protected Note Conservative private wealth, pension funds, risk-averse retail
Class II: Yield Enhancement Maximize periodic coupons in flat or sideways markets $\text{Bond} - \text{Down-and-In Put Option}$ Barrier Reverse Convertible (BRC), Phoenix Autocallable Income-seeking wealth clients willing to take downside equity tail risk
Class III: Participation Capture linear or asymmetric upside without cap $\text{Underlying Asset} + \text{Put Spread} / \text{Replicating Forward}$ Tracker Certificate, Outperformance Note, Twin-Win Note Bullish investors seeking enhanced delta without paying option decay
Class IV: Exotics & Hybrids Leverage, correlation arbitrage, curve slope monetization Multi-asset options, CMS curve spreads, default baskets Worst-Of Autocall, CMS Steepener, CLN, Turbo Warrant Sophisticated family offices, hedge funds, aggressive private clients
MiFID II & PRIIPs Compliance Rule
Under European MiFID II regulations, all structured products distributed to non-institutional clients must include a standardized Packaged Retail and Insurance-based Investment Products (PRIIPs) KID. This document specifies the Summary Risk Indicator (SRI 1 to 7), performance scenarios (Favourable, Moderate, Unfavourable, Stress), and total reduction in yield (RIY) resulting from structuring fees.

The Master Pricing Identity

Mathematical DNA

Every structured product, regardless of complexity or underlying asset class, obeys the Fundamental Theorem of Structuring:

The Structuring Replication Formula
$$\text{Price}_{\text{Structured Note}} = \text{ZCB}(0, T) \times \left(1 + \text{Funding Spread}\right) + \sum_{i=1}^M w_i \cdot \text{Option}_i(S, K_i, T) + \text{Desk Margin}$$

When a bank sells a $\$100$ par structured note at inception ($t = 0$), the $\$100$ cash received is carved into three distinct economic buckets:

1. The Zero-Coupon Bond (ZCB) Leg

To guarantee $\$100$ redemption at maturity $T$, the desk buys (or credits to the bank treasury) a Zero-Coupon Bond maturing at $\$100$:

$$\text{PV}_{\text{ZCB}} = \frac{100}{\left(1 + r + s\right)^T} = 100 \cdot e^{-(r + s) T}$$

where $r$ is the benchmark risk-free rate (e.g., SOFR or €STR swap rate) and $s$ is the bank's unsecured term funding spread.

2. The Available Option Budget

Whatever capital remains after purchasing the ZCB and deducting bank fees constitutes the Derivative Option Budget:

$$\text{Option Budget} = 100 - \text{PV}_{\text{ZCB}} - \text{Desk Margin}$$

This budget is handed to the exotics desk to purchase upside calls, barrier options, or subsidize guaranteed coupons.

Structuring Rule of Thumb: The Interest Rate Engine
Notice how sensitive the Option Budget is to rates! When rates are $1\%$, a 3-year ZCB costs $\$94.26$, leaving only $\$3.74$ for options. When rates rise to $5\%$, the ZCB costs $\$84.44$, ballooning the Option Budget to $\$13.56$ (a $260\%$ increase!). This is why capital-protected notes thrive during high-rate regimes.

Legal Wrappers & Structural Architecture

Documentation & Risk

A structured payoff cannot float in a vacuum. It must be packaged inside a legally enforceable regulatory wrapper. The wrapper dictates whether the note carries bank credit risk, how it is taxed, whether it settles via Euroclear, and what happens in a bank resolution.

Wrapper Type Legal Structure Credit Risk Source Bail-in Subject? Typical Market Use
EMTN (Euro Medium Term Note) Senior unsecured debt issuance program Issuing Bank (Bail-inable) YES (BRRD) $85\%$ of institutional & private wealth structured notes
Exchange Traded Certificate / Warrant Securitized bearer debt derivative Issuing Bank YES Retail public offerings listed on Euronext, Boerse Frankfurt, SIX
Structured Deposit Bank deposit linked to index/rates Issuing Bank (FDIC / DGS Insured) NO (up to €100k/£85k) Conservative retail, corporate treasury seeking capital guarantee
Structured UCITS Fund / SICAV Ring-fenced open-ended collective vehicle Collateral Basket + Swap Counterparty NO (Off-balance sheet) Regulated European institutions, pension funds, UCITS mandates
SPV (Special Purpose Vehicle) Repack Bankruptcy-remote vehicle (Jersey/Lux/Ireland) High-Grade Collateral (Bunds, Gilts) NO (Bankruptcy Remote) Institutional bespoke repackaging, sovereign wealth funds

The BRRD Bail-In Framework & The Lehman Lesson

Prior to September 2008, retail investors purchasing structured notes treated capital protection as "risk-free". When Lehman Brothers filed for bankruptcy, investors discovered that a $100\%$ capital-protected Lehman note was simply senior unsecured debt of Lehman Brothers Holdings Inc. — resulting in recovery rates below $20\text{ cents on the dollar}$.

The BRRD Bail-In Cascade

Under the European Bank Recovery and Resolution Directive (BRRD), if a systemic bank faces insolvency, resolution authorities write down or convert liabilities into equity in strict order:

  1. Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1)
  2. Additional Tier 1 (AT1 Contingent Convertibles)
  3. Tier 2 Subordinated Debt
  4. Senior Non-Preferred & Senior Unsecured Debt (EMTN Structured Notes)
SPV Repacks as the Safe Haven

For institutional clients who refuse bank bail-in risk, structuring desks deploy Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs).

The SPV issues notes to the investor and uses the proceeds to buy AAA-rated sovereign debt (German Bunds, US Treasuries) as collateral. The bank only provides an OTC derivative swap to deliver the payoff. If the bank fails, the collateral belongs strictly to the noteholders.

Credit Rating Distinction: Issuer vs Issue
Always verify whether a structured note has its own debt rating or relies entirely on the Issuer's Long-Term Senior Debt Rating. Most EMTN structured notes are unrated individually; their creditworthiness is identical to the bank's senior credit default swap (CDS) spread.

Desk Economics & Secondary Market Lifecycle

Trading Desk P&L

How does an investment banking structuring desk generate tens of millions in annual P&L? The revenue model is multi-dimensional and spans the entire lifecycle of the product.

1. Upfront Structuring & Distribution Fee

When a $\$100\text{M}$ campaign of Phoenix Autocallables is sold, the bank typically embeds a $2.5\% - 3.5\%$ total upfront load into the note price ($100\%$ issue price, but manufactured for $\$96.50$).

  • Distributor Retrocession: $1.5\% - 2.5\%$ paid to the private bank / wealth advisor as sales commission.
  • Arrangement Margin: $0.5\% - 1.0\%$ retained directly by the structuring desk.
2. Internal Derivative Flow Margin

The structuring desk sources options internally from the exotics trading desk.

  • The exotics desk quotes an OTC option package at the mid-market price with a built-in bid/ask markup.
  • Client-sold volatility (e.g. puts in BRCs) is acquired at cheap implied vol, giving the trading book valuable short gamma and skew hedging flow.

Secondary Market Quoting & Spread Mechanics

Unlike public exchange-traded equities, structured notes trade almost exclusively over-the-counter (OTC) where the issuing bank acts as the exclusive market maker.

Secondary Market Pricing Formula
$$\text{Bid Price}_{\text{Secondary}}(t) = \text{Fair Market Value}(t) - \frac{1}{2}\text{Bid/Ask Spread} - \text{Remaining Unamortized Fees} - \text{Unwind Friction}$$
Lifecycle Phase Typical Bid/Ask Spread Pricing Behavior & Client Impact
Primary Subscription ($t = 0$) $0.0\%$ (Fixed at $100\%$ Par) Upfront fees embedded in the note. Fair value on day 1 is typically $97.0\% - 97.5\%$.
Early Life ($t < 6\text{ Months}$) $1.00\% - 1.50\%$ If an investor liquidates immediately, they suffer the full upfront fee load + market maker bid haircut (the "car leaving the lot" depreciation).
Mid Life ($t \approx 1 - 3\text{ Years}$) $0.50\% - 1.00\%$ Bid price tracks fair value of ZCB + surviving options. Bid/ask spreads tighten as maturity approaches.
Near Maturity / Autocall Date $0.20\% - 0.50\%$ Spreads narrow to money market levels. Price snaps toward redemption par if autocall trigger is assured.
Clean vs Dirty Secondary Prices
Just like corporate bonds, structured notes with periodic coupons quote on a clean price basis on exchanges, while retail wealth statements often display the dirty price ($\text{Clean Price} + \text{Accrued Coupon}$). For autocallables with memory coupons, accrued coupons can represent up to $10\% - 20\%$ of the note value!

Structuring & Sales Desk Interview Flashcards

Below are 6 real questions asked in S&T Structuring and Derivatives Sales interviews at BNP Paribas, Goldman Sachs, and Barclays. Click any card to reveal the desk-level answer:

Q1: "Decompose a 100% Capital-Protected Note with 80% equity participation into vanilla building blocks." ▼
Desk Answer:
The product decomposes into two primary components:
  1. Zero-Coupon Bond (ZCB): Buy a ZCB maturing at $100\%$ par on date $T$. In a $4\%$ rate world over 3 years, this costs $\$100 / (1.04)^3 \approx \$88.90$.
  2. At-the-Money Call Option: With the remaining budget ($\$100 - \$88.90 - \text{Fee} = \$9.10$), buy vanilla ATM European calls on the equity index. If an ATM 3-year call costs $\$11.38$ per unit, your $\$9.10$ budget buys $\frac{9.10}{11.38} = 80\%$ participation.
Total package: $\text{ZCB}_{100\%} + 0.80 \times \text{Call}(K = S_0, T)$.
Q2: "Why do investment banks issue structured notes instead of plain-vanilla senior bonds?" ▼
Desk Answer:
Banks issue structured notes because of funding arbitrage and margin capture:
  • Sub-Wholesale Funding: Institutional wholesale bond buyers demand a credit spread of $\text{SOFR} + 120\text{ bps}$. Retail private wealth clients buying structured notes are focused on the headline coupon ($10\%$) or equity participation, effectively accepting sub-benchmark funding ($\text{SOFR} + 30\text{ bps}$).
  • Upfront Fee Retention: Vanilla bonds pay underwriting fees of $\approx 0.20\% - 0.40\%$. Structured notes generate $1.5\% - 3.5\%$ in upfront desk margins and retrocessions.
  • Internal Derivatives Synergy: The bank captures both the funding spread on the treasury side and the bid/ask margin on the equity derivatives trading desk.
Q3: "Explain what happens to an EMTN structured note under the BRRD bail-in regime if the issuing bank fails." ▼
Desk Answer:
Under the European Bank Recovery and Resolution Directive (BRRD), EMTN structured notes rank as Senior Unsecured Debt. If the bank fails:
  • They are not covered by the €100,000 national deposit guarantee scheme (which only protects structured deposits).
  • Resolution authorities possess the statutory power to permanently write down the principal of the notes or convert them into equity shares of the troubled bank to absorb losses after equity and subordinated debt are wiped out.
  • Consequently, structured note investors bear 100% issuer credit risk regardless of how well the underlying stock or index performed.
Q4: "What happens to the available Option Budget if interest rates drop from 5% to 1%?" ▼
Desk Answer:
The Option Budget collapses drastically:
  • At $5\%$ risk-free rates over a 5-year tenor, the discounted ZCB cost is $\frac{100}{(1.05)^5} = \$78.35$, leaving an enormous Option Budget of $\$19.65$ (assuming $2\%$ fees).
  • At $1\%$ risk-free rates, the ZCB cost surges to $\frac{100}{(1.01)^5} = \$95.15$, leaving an Option Budget of only $\$2.85$.
  • Result: It becomes mathematically impossible to offer attractive capital-protected equity notes in zero/low-rate environments. Desks are forced to pivot toward Yield Enhancement products (Barrier Reverse Convertibles) where investors sell options to generate yield.
Q5: "A client buys a 3Y Phoenix Autocall at 100% and wants to unwind after 1 month when spot hasn't moved. Why is the bid price 95%?" ▼
Desk Answer:
The $5\%$ discount is driven by three institutional factors:
  1. Upfront Fee Friction: The note was sold at $100\%$ but manufactured at $\$96.50$ (including $2.5\%$ distributor retrocession and $1.0\%$ desk fee). That upfront fee is sunk and cannot be recouped.
  2. Bid/Ask Spread Haircut: The market maker quotes a $1.00\% - 1.50\%$ bid/ask spread on secondary trading to protect against inventory holding risk.
  3. Trading Desk Unwind Costs: The exotics desk must unwind its physical delta hedge and close out its OTC option position at bid prices.
Q6: "What is the structural difference between an EMTN and an SPV Repack note in terms of credit risk?" ▼
Desk Answer:
  • EMTN: The credit risk is 100% tied to the bank's balance sheet. If the bank defaults, the investor becomes an unsecured creditor in resolution.
  • SPV Repack: The note is issued by a bankruptcy-remote special purpose vehicle. The investor's principal is collateralized by segregated, high-grade assets (e.g. German Bunds or US Treasuries held in custody). If the arranging bank defaults, the collateral is liquidated to repay noteholders. The investor only has derivative counterparty risk on the swap payoff, not on their principal capital.
