Equity-Linked & Autocallables

Deconstruct the dominant retail and wealth management structures: Phoenix and Athena autocallables, Barrier Reverse Convertibles, memory coupons, and worst-of correlation.

The Wealth Management Juggernaut

Equity-Linked Ecosystem

Equity-linked structured products account for over 70% of global retail and private banking structured issuance. They bridge the gap between traditional fixed income (which offers limited yield in low-rate regimes) and outright equity investing (which carries full unbuffered downside exposure).

🎯 Target Macro View
  • Moderately Bullish, Flat, or Mildly Bearish: The client does not anticipate a strong runaway rally (which would favor direct equity ownership) nor an outright market collapse below the 60%–70% protection barrier.
  • Yield Hunting: Seeking double-digit income ($8\% - 14\%\text{ p.a.}$) in flat markets by monetizing equity index or single-stock implied volatility.
  • Reinvestment Appetite: Willing to accept early redemption if markets rise (autocall recycling).
⚖️ The Asymmetric Trade-Off
  • Asymmetric Payoff: Capped upside return (limited to the guaranteed or memory coupon) in exchange for deep conditional downside buffer (protection barrier typically at $60\% - 70\%$).
  • Tail Risk Exposure: If the underlying breaches the barrier at maturity, capital protection vanishes completely, and the investor participates $1:1$ in the equity downturn from initial strike.
  • Dividend Forfeiture: The bank captures all dividends paid by the underlying stocks to fund the coupon yield.

The Equity-Linked Product Spectrum

From static reverse convertibles to multi-asset step-down autocallables, each product targets a specific risk/reward profile:

The "Step-Down" Innovation
Modern autocallables almost universally incorporate a Step-Down Autocall Barrier (e.g., $100\%$ at Year 1, $95\%$ at Year 2, $90\%$ at Year 3, $85\%$ at Year 4). Step-down barriers significantly increase the probability of early redemption in stagnant or mildly declining markets, drastically shortening expected duration and reducing client frustration.

Mathematical Payoff Formulations

Quantitative Mechanics

1. Barrier Reverse Convertible (BRC) Payoff

BRC Terminal Payoff Formula
$$\text{Payoff}(S_T) = \text{Coupon} + \begin{cases} 100\% & \text{if } \min_{t \in [0, T]} S_t > B \quad (\text{or if } S_T \ge S_0) \\ \frac{S_T}{S_0} \times 100\% & \text{if } \min_{t \in [0, T]} S_t \le B \quad \text{and } S_T < S_0 \end{cases}$$

2. Phoenix Autocallable Schedule & Memory Feature

On each observation date $t_k \in \{t_1, t_2, \dots, t_N\}$:

Autocall & Memory Coupon Rules
$$\begin{aligned} \text{\bf 1. Autocall Test:} & \quad \text{If } S_{t_k} \ge K_{\text{call}}(t_k) \implies \text{Redeem } 100\% + \text{Coupon}(t_k) + \sum_{j \in \text{unpaid}} \text{Coupon}(t_j) \quad [\text{Product Terminates}] \\ \text{\bf 2. Coupon Test:} & \quad \text{If } S_{t_k} \ge B_{\text{coupon}} \implies \text{Pay } \text{Coupon}(t_k) + \sum_{j \in \text{unpaid}} \text{Coupon}(t_j) \\ \text{\bf 3. Memory Carry:} & \quad \text{If } S_{t_k} < B_{\text{coupon}} \implies \text{Coupon is not paid, but recorded in Memory.} \end{aligned}$$

In Scenario B, spot dips below the coupon barrier on Semester 2 and Semester 3. Under a classic note without memory, those coupons are lost forever. Decomposition & Replication Engine

Desk Engineering

In an institutional structuring interview, you must be able to decompose any equity-linked payoff into vanilla options on the whiteboard in 30 seconds.

Decomposition of a Barrier Reverse Convertible
$$\text{BRC} = \text{Straight Bond} - \text{Down-and-In Put Option}$$
  • Buy a fixed-rate bond paying par plus coupon.
  • Sell an OTC Down-and-In Put Option with strike $K = S_0$ and continuous barrier $B = 65\%$.
  • The client receives the put option premium upfront, which is converted into the enhanced periodic coupon.
Decomposition of a Phoenix Autocall
$$\text{Phoenix} = \text{ZCB} + \sum_{k=1}^N \text{Digital Calls} - \text{DI-Put}$$
  • A portfolio of Bermudan digital/binary call options paying the periodic coupon if $S_{t_k} \ge B_{\text{coupon}}$.
  • A series of early redemption binary calls terminating the note if $S_{t_k} \ge K_{\text{call}}$.
  • A short European or American Down-and-In Put governing final maturity downside capital risk below $B_{\text{prot}}$.

The "Worst-Of" Correlation Trade

Why do over $80\%$ of modern autocalls feature a basket of 3 or 4 stocks (e.g. LVMH, ASML, TotalEnergies, BNP Paribas) instead of a single stock?

Worst-Of Performance Formula
$$\text{Underlying Basket Performance}(t) = \min_{i \in \{1, 2, \dots, N\}} \left( \frac{S_i(t)}{S_i(0)} \right)$$
The "Free Lunch" Illusion

A single-stock Phoenix on Euro Stoxx 50 might offer a 6.5% coupon. By switching to a 3-stock Worst-Of basket, the offered coupon surges to 12.0%.

Retail wealth investors perceive this as a "free lunch" because all 3 companies are blue-chip mega-caps. In reality, the investor is selling a basket correlation option.

The Mathematical Reality of Correlation

The price of a worst-of put option is strictly decreasing in cross-asset correlation $\rho_{i,j}$:

$$\frac{\partial \text{Worst-Of Put}}{\partial \rho_{i,j}} < 0$$

If correlation is low or negative, the probability that at least one stock drops below the $65\%$ barrier surges toward $100\%$, drastically increasing the put premium collected.

The Idiosyncratic Risk Trap
In a 4-stock Worst-Of basket, 3 stocks can rally $+50\%$, but if the 4th stock suffers an accounting fraud or profit warning and crashes $-55\%$, the investor suffers a severe capital loss. The investor has maximum downside exposure to the laggard with zero benefit from the outperformers.

Trading Desk Greeks & Dynamic Hedging

Risk Management

When an investment bank issues $\$500\text{M}$ of autocallables, the exotics trading desk holds the opposing position: the desk is long volatility, long correlation, and short downside puts. Managing this book requires navigating violent nonlinear Greeks.

Delta ($\Delta$) Dynamics & Pin Risk
  • Far Above Call Barrier: The note is almost certain to call. Effective duration drops to zero, and the delta hedge approaches zero.
  • Near Downside Barrier at Maturity: The payoff is discontinuous. If spot is $65.1\%$ with 2 days to maturity, delta explodes toward infinity ($\lim_{T-t \to 0} \Delta \to \infty$).
  • Pin Risk: If spot crosses the barrier back and forth, the trader must aggressively buy high and sell low, suffering brutal whipsaw slippage.
Gamma ($\Gamma$) Sign Inversion
  • Autocallable books undergo violent gamma flips.
  • When spot is comfortably between the coupon barrier and autocall barrier, gamma is positive or neutral.
  • As spot declines toward the knock-in barrier ($65\%$), gamma becomes deeply negative. The desk must sell shares into a crashing market to stay delta-neutral, amplifying market selloffs.
Vega ($\nu$) & Volatility Skew
  • Investor is Short Vega: As market volatility spikes, the probability of hitting both the downside barrier and missing autocall triggers rises. The note value declines.
  • Desk is Long Vega: The desk benefits from high implied vol at inception because it sells expensive options.
  • Vanna-Volga Exposure: Downside OTM put options trade at a steep volatility skew premium. If skew steepens, barrier breach probability spikes.
Dividend & Repo Risk
  • Dividend Funding: The desk finances high coupons by forfeiting stock dividends. If companies cut dividends (e.g. during COVID-19 in 2020), future cashflows collapse.
  • The structured desk loses millions if implied dividends drop: $\frac{\partial V}{\partial q} < 0$.
  • This risk prompted the creation of Decrement Indices (e.g. Euro Stoxx 50 Equal Weight 50 Pt Decrement), which synthetically deduct a fixed dividend to eliminate dividend risk for the desk.
March 2020 COVID Autocall Crisis
In March 2020, global equity markets plummeted, implied volatility doubled, and European companies cancelled dividends. Investment banks (notably Natixis and BNP Paribas) suffered hundreds of millions in P&L losses on their autocall hedging books due to the combined blow of negative gamma, dividend cancellations, and correlation breakdowns.

Equity Structuring Interview Flashcards

Test your mastery of equity structuring with these 6 high-probability interview questions. Click any card to reveal the desk answer:

Q1: "What is the structural difference between an Athena (Express) and a Phoenix Autocallable?" ▼
Desk Answer:
  • Phoenix: Pays periodic conditional coupons (e.g. quarterly or semi-annually) whenever spot is above the coupon barrier, often with a memory feature. The investor receives cashflows throughout the product life even if no early call occurs.
  • Athena (Express): Pays no periodic coupons. Cashflow only occurs upon redemption. If autocalled, it pays $100\%$ par plus a cumulative bonus (e.g., $+10\%$ per year elapsed). If held to maturity without autocall, it pays par (if barrier intact) or suffers capital loss. Athena is favored in tax jurisdictions where capital gains are taxed lower than dividend/coupon income.
Q2: "Why does adding a step-down barrier reduce the offered coupon rate?" ▼
Desk Answer:
A step-down barrier (e.g. $100\% \to 95\% \to 90\%$) makes it much easier for the note to autocall in later periods.
  • Because early redemption is more likely, the expected life (duration) of the note shortens significantly.
  • A shorter expected duration means the bank collects less total put option premium and captures fewer dividend distributions from the underlying stocks.
  • Therefore, the bank has less option budget to fund coupons, resulting in a slightly lower headline coupon ($10\%$ vs $11.5\%$), but offering significantly greater safety to the investor.
Q3: "A trader has a short position on a client's Phoenix note. Spot is hovering at 60.5% with 2 days to maturity (barrier at 60%). Describe the Greek profile." ▼
Desk Answer:
This is the trader's worst nightmare (pin risk & short gamma squeeze):
  • Discontinuous Delta: If spot stays above $60\%$, redemption is $100\% \implies \Delta = 0$. If spot drops to $59.9\%$, redemption is $59.9\% \implies \Delta = 1.0$. The delta curve jumps from $0$ to $1$ over a few basis points, making $\Delta$ massive and unstable.
  • Negative Gamma Spike: As $T - t \to 0$, gamma approaches negative infinity. The trader must aggressively sell shares as the market falls below $60\%$ and buy shares if it ticks back above $60\%$.
  • Desk Resolution: In practice, traders smooth the barrier internally using a narrow put spread or over-hedge with liquid exchange-traded vanilla puts to avoid getting pinned.
Q4: "Why does a 3-stock Worst-Of basket note offer a much higher coupon than a single-stock note?" ▼
Desk Answer:
The investor is short a correlation option:
  • In a worst-of structure, the payoff is governed by $\min(S_1/S_{0,1}, S_2/S_{0,2}, S_3/S_{0,3})$.
  • The probability that at least one stock drops by $35\%$ and breaches the barrier is significantly higher than the probability of any single given stock doing so.
  • Mathematically, $\frac{\partial \text{Worst-Of Put}}{\partial \rho} < 0$. Lower correlation between the stocks expands the dispersion of outcomes and inflates the value of the short put option sold by the investor.
  • The desk monetizes this rich put premium to pay a much higher coupon ($12\%$ vs $6\%$).
Q5: "How does a sudden dividend cancellation affect the price of an existing equity-linked structured note?" ▼
Desk Answer:
A dividend cut has two distinct effects:
  • Forward Price Rises: Forward price is $F = S \cdot e^{(r - q)T}$. If dividend yield $q$ drops, the forward price of the stock rises. This mechanically increases the probability of hitting the autocall barrier, which is bullish for the note.
  • Trading Desk P&L Collapse: For the structuring desk that issued the note, dividends were pledged to fund the promised coupons. If the stock cancels its dividend, the desk suffers an unhedged cashflow deficit. This is why desks frequently hedge dividend risk using single-stock dividend futures on Eurex.
Q6: "What is the difference between an American barrier and a European barrier in a Barrier Reverse Convertible?" ▼
Desk Answer:
  • American Barrier (Continuous / Intraday): The barrier is monitored continuously every second of every trading day until maturity. If spot touches or falls below the barrier at any point in the 3-year life, the barrier is permanently broken ("knocked in"), even if the stock immediately rebounds $+50\%$ the next day.
  • European Barrier (At Maturity Only): The barrier is observed strictly once: at the market close on the final valuation date $T$. What happens intraday during the 3 years is completely irrelevant.
  • Pricing Impact: American barriers carry higher risk of breach, so the sold put is more valuable $\implies$ American BRCs offer higher coupons than European BRCs by $\approx 100 - 200\text{ bps}$.
